**Envelope close-up and honest inhibitor notes**

- Phase envelope close-up: a lean gas (mostly methane) has a narrow envelope near −70 °C, and its bubble and dew curves run almost on top of each other. The hydrate curve and the water dew line stretch the plot from −200 to +200 °C, so the envelope looked like a single line. When the envelope fills less than a third of the plot, a second plot now shows the envelope alone, close up, with its critical point, cricondenbar and cricondentherm. The curved, closed dome of a richer gas comes from its heavier components (C4+, C6+, C7+): add them from your gas analysis.
- Hydrate inhibitor: the app now gives the temperature below which the inhibited free water freezes. Colder than that, hydrates form from ice, and the inhibitor cannot lower them. The app used to say "0 K lower" there, with the GPSA correlation (which leaves out freezing) beside it. The freezing point is DWSIM's (NRTL water activity). It is a few K warmer than measured (−11 °C against −15 °C at 20 wt % methanol), which errs on the safe side.
