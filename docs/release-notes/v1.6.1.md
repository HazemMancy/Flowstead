**What's fixed in 1.6.1**

**Depressuring runs.** A vessel holding water with its hydrocarbons stopped after a few seconds (DWSIM's three-phase flash jumps near the water's phase boundary and the pressure solve gave up), and the orifice sizing then kept enlarging the orifice until it timed out. The blowdown now rides over those jumps, every flash reuses one prepared DWSIM stream (the same results, four to six times faster), and the wall can no longer be cooled below the fluid by a strong boiling film. A typical separator with free water now sizes its orifice in about a minute on a server; on the phone allow a few minutes.

**Relief valves give the valve.** The result now leads with the API 526 orifice letter and the smallest standard valve for it (for example 3K4: 3 in inlet × K × 4 in outlet), then the selected and required areas with their equivalent diameters in mm and inches. When more than one T is needed, the right letter is shown for the several valves.

**Straight stream lines.** Connections are drawn as one straight line. A dragged stream or unit settles on the grid and lines up with what it connects to, so its lines come out level or plumb.

**Pipeline pressurization (new), as HYSYS's template.** Safety analysis → Pressurization: choose the supply stream, the pipe segments to fill (and add sections by hand for a network), the starting pressure and the control valve (Cv and opening) or restriction orifice, or let Flowstead size the orifice to your target time. You get the time to reach the target pressure and the pressure, gas and wall temperatures and flow against time, with the hottest gas and the coldest gas the valve lets in (Joule–Thomson) against the minimum design metal temperature.

**Pipelines can be protected and blown down.** A relief valve or a depressuring case can protect a pipe segment: its volume and surface come from its inside diameter and length (no heads), and its wall thickness from its schedule.

**Relief valves use the ideal-gas k as API 520 asks.** k now comes straight from DWSIM's ideal-gas heat capacity of each component, so the warning that the real-gas Cp/Cv was used no longer appears (it came from a low-pressure flash that could fail). The real-gas Cp/Cv at relieving pressure is higher (1.54 against 1.28 for a natural gas at 77 bar) and would undersize the valve, which is why API 520 uses the ideal-gas value.

**Bubble and dew points on every stream.** The stream's Overall properties now list the bubble point and dew point temperatures at its pressure and the bubble point and dew point pressures at its temperature, each from DWSIM's own flash (as HYSYS's stream properties). A point that does not exist, such as a dew point above the cricondentherm, is left blank. As in HYSYS, a vapour fraction of 0 specifies the bubble point (all liquid, the colder end) and 1 the dew point (all vapour, the warmer end).

**Relief valves on the flowsheet.** The PSV in the Piping palette is now available. Drop it on the flowsheet and it is a Safety analysis relief valve: tap it to choose what it protects, its set pressure and scenarios, and size it, with the same fields and result as in Safety analysis. A dashed line shows what it protects. It takes no part in the solve, so it never stops one.

**Separator sizing, with its internals.** In a separator's editor, "Size this vessel" sizes it from the solved flows: vertical or horizontal, two- or three-phase, with the mist eliminator (none, wire mesh, vane pack, demisting cyclones) and inlet device you choose. You get the diameter, the tangent-to-tangent length, the L/D, the allowable and actual gas velocity, the liquid levels (LLL, NLL, HLL and the oil–water interface), the mist eliminator area and the inlet, gas and liquid nozzle sizes. Every default (K factors, times, droplet sizes, momentum limits) comes from the published practice (GPSA, Svrcek & Monnery, API 12J) and can be replaced by your company's. Sizing for the other equipment follows.

**Sizing for the other equipment, and an engineering data library.** Heat exchangers, heaters, coolers and air coolers give their heat-transfer area from the solved duty and temperatures, with the LMTD, the F correction and the number of shells in series; U comes from a library of typical values by service (with the area over the whole range) or your own, and heaters and coolers take the utility's temperatures (steam, cooling water or air by default). Pumps, compressors and expanders give the driver: the API 610 or 617 margin and the next standard IEC motor. Tanks give their volume and dimensions from a residence time. Every library value shows where it comes from and can be replaced.
