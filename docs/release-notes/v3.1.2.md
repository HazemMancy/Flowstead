**New example: instrument air with a saturator and the wet air receiver's PSV**

- Help → Examples → "Instrument air: saturator and wet air receiver with its PSV". Dry compressed air (500 Nm³/h at 40 °C, 9 barg) is saturated with water in a mixer, with more water than it can hold. It settles in the wet air receiver V-100 (1.29 m × 1.84 m, vertical, 0.3 m of condensate), with wet air out at the top and condensate at the bottom.
- The example comes with PSV-100, set at 10 barg, and two scenarios: fire and blocked outlet. Open Safety and press Size: the fire boils the receiver's water (API 521 wetted fire, D102) and needs a 1D2 valve, the same as HYSYS. The blocked outlet relieves the whole feed, about 650 kg/h of wet air, and governs with a 1E2 valve.
- A two-phase separator now solves when free water forms. Its liquid outlet carries both liquids, as in HYSYS. Before, any separator that condensed water (a wet air receiver, a knock-out drum on wet gas) stopped with "connect another stream to the second liquid phase outlet". A three-phase separator still sends the water to its own outlet.
- Liquid densities no longer change at random from one solve to the next. The COSTALD rule for hydrocarbon liquids (3.0) could, at times, also be applied to free water calculated at the same moment on another thread. In example 4 the water came out at 994.2 or 990.6 kg/m³ depending on the run. It is now always 990.6.
