**More accurate NRTL and UNIQUAC energy balances; liquids no longer reported as vapour**

- NRTL and UNIQUAC now take liquid enthalpy and heat capacity from each compound's experimental liquid heat capacity, as DWSIM 10 does. Before, liquid water came out with a heat capacity of 5.35 kJ/kg·K instead of 4.18, so liquid heating and cooling duties with these packages were about 28 % too high. Stream enthalpies are now measured from liquid at 25 °C, so their values look different; duties and temperatures are what changed in substance. Example: the ethylene-oxide hydration reactor now releases 98 kJ per mol reacted, against 96 from published heats of formation (before: 106).
- A stream with no flow (for example a reactor's liquid outlet when everything leaves as vapour) now shows its temperature, pressure and zero flow only, instead of placeholder compositions and properties.
- DWSIM files saved by DWSIM 10 now open with their column feeds on the right trays.
- A liquid mixture of light and heavy hydrocarbons (for example propane with pentane, hexane and decane at 10 bar) could be reported as a vapour, with no liquid density and the wrong enthalpy. A single-phase result is now labelled by the fluid itself, as Flowstead already did for dense gases: that condensate is a liquid at 659.8 kg/m³ (reference equation of state: 660.1).
