**What's new in 1.6.0: safety analysis and more of HYSYS (kij, inhibitors, variable-speed machines, PVT regression)**

Menu → **Safety analysis**. Every fluid property comes from DWSIM at relieving conditions; ThermoDock does the standards' arithmetic and shows which equation it used.

**Relief valves (API 520 / 521 / 526).** Add a relief valve to a separator or tank, give its set pressure and the vessel's dimensions, and list its scenarios:
- **Fire**: heat input on the wetted area (Q = 43.2 F A^0.82 kW, or 70.9 without drainage and fire-fighting), boiling the vessel's liquid at the relieving pressure; or the unwetted-wall method for a gas-filled vessel.
- **Blocked outlet**: the vessel's whole feed, from the solved flowsheet.
- **Control valve failure**: the flow through a fully open valve from its upstream pressure.
- **Thermal expansion** of blocked-in liquid.
- **Other**: any flow and temperature you give.

Each case is sized for vapour, liquid, two-phase (omega method, isentropic flash as API 520 prefers) or flashing liquid as the fluid demands; the largest governs and ThermoDock picks the API 526 orifice (D to T, or several valves).

**Inlet and outlet lines (API 520 Part II).** Pick the lines' nominal size and schedule: the inlet pressure loss at the valve's rated flow is checked against 3 % of set pressure, and the built-up back pressure against 10 % (conventional), 30 % (balanced bellows) or 50 % (pilot).

**Depressuring (API 521), like HYSYS's blowdown.** Blow a vessel down through a restriction orifice. The vapour and the liquid are tracked as separate zones with their own temperatures; the wall conducts through its thickness, wetted and dry sections apart, with natural-convection and nucleate-boiling coefficients (or your own, or adiabatic). You get pressure, vapour, liquid and both wall-surface temperatures, liquid holdup and flow against time, with the orifice sized to reach 6.9 barg (or half the starting pressure) within 15 minutes if you don't give one. A warning flags temperatures below −29 °C (check the minimum design metal temperature). Fire during depressuring is an option.

Like HYSYS, ThermoDock suggests the relief scenarios from the flowsheet: fire, the vessel's blocked outlet, each control valve feeding it failing open (upstream state from the solve, its rated Cv) and thermal expansion when it holds only liquid. Add, edit or drop them.

**Valve library.** Choose a control valve's type (globe, cage, angle, ball, segmented ball, butterfly, eccentric rotary plug) and ThermoDock uses its typical IEC 60534 FL and xT for sizing, cavitation and choked flow; type the vendor's figures to override them.

**Variable-speed pumps and compressors.** Give the curve's speed, add curves at other speeds and set the speed the machine runs at: ThermoDock reads the map there by the affinity laws, blending between the two nearest speed lines (surge and stonewall move with it). Or, as in HYSYS, give the outlet pressure instead and ThermoDock finds the speed. Curves can also be a least-squares polynomial fit of your points, as HYSYS fits them, instead of straight lines.

**Hydrate inhibitors.** On the phase envelope, choose methanol, MEG, DEG or TEG and its strength in the free water. The inhibitor enters the hydrate equilibrium through the water's activity, as in HYSYS: the inhibited hydrate curve is solved and drawn beside the uninhibited one, and ThermoDock solves how much inhibitor the stream's water needs to stay out of hydrates at its temperature.

**PVT regression.** In the PVT sheet, type your lab measurements (saturation pressure, CCE relative volume, density or liquid dropout, DL Bo or Rs) and ThermoDock tunes the plus fraction's critical constants and its kij with methane to match them, showing the fit before and after. Apply the tuned constants to the fluid when you are satisfied (undo goes back).

**Binary interaction parameters.** On the fluid package screen, Edit kij lists every pair with DWSIM's value; type your own (HYSYS's, for instance) to replace it.
